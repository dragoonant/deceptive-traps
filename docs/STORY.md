# Brassmoor: story bible

> Companion to `DESIGN.md`. Chapter story, one ending, told through dialogue
> with portraits, illustrated cutscenes and notes found in the manor.

## Logline

A Keeper who cannot fight defends a clockwork manor from the people who want
its engine, and learns that the house has been keeping her alive.

## Tone

Gothic, candlelit, a little absurd. The traps are Rube Goldberg slapstick, not
gore: defeated intruders are knocked senseless and dragged off by their friends.
The story underneath is sincere: grief, debt, and what we owe the people who
made us.

## The world

* **Brassmoor** is a manor alone on a wet northern moor. Behind its brick walls
  are gears, pistons, counterweights and speaking tubes. At its heart, in the
  Boiler Room, beats the **Heartspring**: a perpetual-motion mainspring that
  nobody else has been able to build.
* **The Gearwright Guild** licenses every invention in the country and takes a
  cut. Corvel never paid. On paper, the Guild owns half the house.
* **The Order of the Still Hour** is a sect that believes anything that ticks and
  thinks is an abomination.

## Nights (chapter outline)

Each night is one chapter: an illustrated intro, dialogue, 3–5 waves, then a
closing scene. Notes found that night fill in the past.

1. **The First Night.** Rook's crew comes for the "gold doorknobs." The House
   teaches Wren the traps. Jessamy Rook makes her entrance in wave 3. In the
   last wave Sir Oswin Garrick arrives to "inspect the property," is sent
   packing, and at dawn nails the Guild's writ to the door. *(Built in v0.3.)*
2. **The Writ.** Garrick returns with Guild Knights and serves the writ in
   person, between waves. Rook comes back, this time working for the Guild.
   Wren finds the first sign that the House knows more than it says.
3. **The Still Hour.** Mother Hesper's Inquisitors disarm traps (new intruder:
   *Inquisitor*). Hesper calls Wren "the clock's child." Garrick, shaken, starts
   asking the Guild questions.
4. **The Son.** Tobias Corvel climbs in over the walls (new intruder: *Acrobat*)
   looking for his father. Wren can talk him down instead of trapping him. He
   leaves Corvel's last letter: *"If the house still ticks, so does she."*
5. **The Demolition.** Ilse Varga's Sappers blow holes in the walls (new
   intruder: *Sapper*; rooms change mid-night). In the Boiler Room Wren hears
   the Heartspring falter, and her own chest flutters with it.
6. **Fell.** The Guildmaster leads everyone in a siege. The House admits the
   truth: it is Corvel, and the Heartspring is running down because it has been
   keeping two things alive. Corvel chooses Wren: he gives her the last of the
   spring and the house goes still. Fell finds an engine that will never run
   again. At dawn Wren walks out across the moor for the first time.

**Ending (single):** bittersweet. The manor is quiet and the Guild's prize is
worthless. Garrick, who now knows the truth, tears up the writ. In the final
panel, a small brass bird ticks on Wren's shoulder: a last gift from Corvel.

## Foreshadowing in Night 1

* The House says "we" when it means Wren's survival ("we don't fight").
* Corvel's work log in the Boiler Room: *"The fever broke. The second spring
  took."*
* The indenture in the Foyer binds the Keeper "for as long as the house keeps
  time," not for a number of years.
* The Guild letter in the Gallery is addressed to Corvel "or his heirs and
  mechanisms."

## Presentation

* **Illustrated cutscenes:** comic panels rendered from the game's own low-poly
  models and lighting, with hand-lettered captions. Shown at the start and end
  of each night.
* **Dialogue:** a text box with portraits, also rendered from the in-game
  characters. It pauses play. The House has no face: its portrait is a brass
  speaking-tube grille that pulses when it talks.
* **Notes:** glowing pages placed in the rooms. They can be read during setup
  (walk up and press **E**, or tap **Read**). Each night has three.

## Style guide

* Low-poly and stylized: flat-shaded shapes, chunky silhouettes, readable
  colours per faction (Rook's crew greens and red; the Guild steel and crimson;
  the Still Hour bone white and black).
* Brass, soot, candlelight. Each room has its own palette: the Foyer warm
  amber, the Boiler Room furnace orange, the Gallery moonlight blue.
* Characters speak in short lines. No line in a dialogue box is longer than
  two sentences.
